Yes — I’d tighten the whole product around one very specific promise:

> **Langboard helps you say what you actually want to say in another language — then helps you remember it for next time.**

That gives you a coherent premium product instead of “keyboard + flashcards + history + random language-learning features.”

The **keyboard solves the moment**.

That should be the entire product philosophy.

# The core product loop

This is the thing I’d design *everything* around:

```text
You try to say something in real life
          ↓
You get stuck / sound unnatural
          ↓
Langboard helps you express it naturally
          ↓
That moment is captured automatically
          ↓
Langboard decides what was worth learning
          ↓
You review it later in context
          ↓
You can say it yourself next time
```

This is much stronger than:

> “AI language learning app with SRS and translation.”

Because the curriculum is generated from **your real communication needs**.

That is the product.

# 2. REVIEW — SRS, but Langboard-specific

I would keep your SRS, but heavily differentiate it from Anki.

## Review type A — reconstruct your original gap

You originally wrote:

```text
我本来想去但是 I couldn't be bothered anymore
```

Days later:

```text
You were texting a friend:

我本来想去但是 ______

“I couldn't be bothered anymore”

What would you say?
```

User tries:

```text
懒得去了
```

Then:

```text
✓ Nice

Original:
懒得再去了

Your answer works too.
```

That is *excellent* language learning because it recreates a genuine retrieval need.

---

# Review type B — Naturalize

```text
Would you change this?

我没有那个兴趣了
```

User:

```text
我已经没啥兴趣了
```

Then show:

```text
Natural ✓

You usually prefer:
我已经没啥兴趣了
```

Now the user practices native judgment instead of memorizing translations.

---

# Review type C — expression recognition

```text
Your friend texts:

我真服了

Closest meaning?

A. I agree
B. I'm seriously done / unbelievable
C. I'm tired
D. I'm impressed
```

Useful for expressions learned from incoming conversations.

---

# Review type D — contextual selection

```text
Your friend keeps making pointed comments.

Which fits best?

阴阳怪气
被动攻击
消极攻击
不太开心
```

Then teach *why*.

This is particularly valuable because the hard part of advanced language learning isn't knowing words.

It's knowing **which expression belongs here**.

---

# Review type E — register switching

Take something the user already learned:

```text
Tell your friend:
“Could you send that again?”

再发我一下呗
```

Now:

```text
Now say it to your manager.
```

Possible:

```text
可以再发我一下吗？
```

This directly reinforces Langboard's key competency: **socially appropriate expression**.

# SRS should be selective

This is important.

Do **not** automatically turn every Langboard invocation into a card.

That would become overwhelming.

Langboard should calculate something like:

> **Learning Value**

Higher priority if:

* the user got stuck multiple times;
* it's useful across many situations;
* they manually saved it;
* they selected an alternative;
* they corrected Langboard;
* they looked at the explanation;
* they previously failed it;
* it's an expression rather than a random proper noun.

Low priority:

* one-off product name;
* address;
* technical jargon;
* trivial dictionary lookup;
* phrase already used fluently repeatedly.

Premium feeling comes partly from **curation**.
